Nisab, hawl, and debts: when zakat is due

Zakat is due when your net zakatable wealth reaches the nisab and you’ve held it for a lunar year. Here is how each part works.

Nisab: the value of 85 grams of gold. Multiply today’s gold price per gram by 85.

What is the nisab?

The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that makes zakat obligatory. US Zakat Calculator uses the gold standard: 85 grams of gold at the current market price. If your net zakatable wealth is equal to or above the nisab, zakat is due at 2.5%. If it is below, no zakat is due.

Nisab = gold price per gram × 85

Which debts can you deduct?

Only short-term liabilities reduce your zakatable wealth: credit card balances due now and the current month’s bills. Long-term debts like mortgages and student loans are not deducted.

The hawl: your zakat date

The hawl is one lunar (Hijri) year. Your zakat date is the Hijri date when your wealth first reached the nisab, and zakat becomes due on that date each lunar year. A lunar year is about 11 days shorter than a calendar year, so your zakat date moves earlier each year on the Gregorian calendar.
