Zakat on stocks, ETFs, and index funds

How much zakat you owe on shares depends on why you hold them. Long-term investors pay on the company’s zakatable assets; active traders pay on the full market value.

Short answer: for stocks and funds you hold long-term, zakat is 2.5% of the companies’ zakatable assets, not of the full market value. When you can’t look those up, the Fiqh Council of North America allows assuming 30% of market value is zakatable.1 On $10,000 of index funds, that is about $75.

Long-term (passive) investments

Stocks, and funds of stocks such as ETFs and mutual funds, are fractional ownership in businesses. Zakat on a business is 2.5% of its zakatable assets — cash, receivables, and inventory — not its fixed assets like buildings or equipment. A long-term shareholder pays their prorated share at the end of each lunar year.1

If you can’t get these figures for a company, FCNA allows assuming 30% of market value is zakatable, based on recent S&P 500 averages.1

Zakatable value = market value × zakatable percentage

Example

A $58,200 brokerage account in index funds × 30% = $17,460 zakatable. Zakat due at 2.5% is $436.50, if your total wealth meets the nisab.

Active trading

Shares bought to trade frequently are trade goods, so zakat is 2.5% of their full market value at the end of the lunar year: $10,000 of trading stock means $250 of zakat. As a general guideline, FCNA treats an intent to hold for less than 365 days as short-term.1 What matters is your intent for the shares, not whether the company pays dividends.1

Zakatable percentage by ticker

Instead of the flat 30%, you can use a fund’s actual ratio, as FCNA recommends where the data is available. FCNA’s own retirement examples use 24.8% for VOO and 8.24% for SPUS.2 US Zakat Calculator estimates the ratio for each company as current assets ÷ market capitalization, from the latest balance sheet. For ETFs it looks through to the holdings: the weighted average of each holding’s percentage, plus the fund’s cash. Bond and precious-metal funds count at 100%. The data is refreshed weekly and covers the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, and popular Shariah-compliant ETFs.
